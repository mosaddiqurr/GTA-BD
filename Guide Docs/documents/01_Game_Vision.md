# 01 | Game Vision Document

## Product identity
**Working title:** Bangladesh Open World. **Genre:** single-player, non-violent, third-person, open-world exploration/sandbox with eventual narrative investigation. **Primary platform:** web browsers. **Secondary possibility:** installable desktop/mobile builds only after real feasibility tests.

## Vision
Let people experience recognizable Bangladeshi places through a visually distinctive and technically accessible open-world game. The game is a free-roaming experience, not a replica of GTA's violence or criminal gameplay.

## Core player promise
Explore and recognize Bangladesh; walk through believable streets; meet fruit-character locals; use locally authentic transport; enjoy Bangla ambience and later engage in purposeful social-engineering/scam investigations without combat.

## Visual ambition
Professional-quality, art-directed 3D. Prefer smaller scenes with carefully composed geometry, high-quality PBR materials, deliberate lighting, traffic detail, audio and convincing movement over vast generic environments. Stylized realism; visually memorable fruit citizens.

## Audiences and platforms
- Initial: PC/laptop users with a modern browser and capable graphics hardware.
- Later: mid-tier mobile players through adaptive graphics and touch controls after benchmark tests.
- Bangladesh-centered linguistic/cultural identity with international visitors able to use an English-language option later.

## World expansion vision
- **Starting hub:** Mirpur 10, Dhaka, recognizable but selectively compressed into a good playable space.
- **Dhaka growth:** additional neighborhoods, intersections, parks, historic and civic landmarks. Exact future districts decided by recognizability, gameplay and production cost.
- **Destination expansions:** Cox's Bazar (coastal town/beaches) and Saint Martin's Island (island/coastal ecology), represented as distinct regions with unique art, weather, sound and movement needs.
- **Beyond:** other Bangladesh cities and landmarks chosen later, no hard-coded fixed list.
- **Travel design:** the player selects/arrives at regions through transport or a world map. No need to simulate the entire road distance between every city.

## Non-violence and deferred cybersecurity
Shooting, attacks, fighting, weapons and combat missions are out of scope for all planned releases. Scam, phishing, social engineering and citizen safety stories may come later through observation, evidence and dialogue, but no such mission systems are implemented now.

## Experience pillars
1. **Recognition:** recognizable silhouettes and relationships between roads, landmark architecture, metro structures and Bangla signage.
2. **Free movement:** walking and later riding, driving and interacting without constant menu transitions.
3. **Living environment:** pedestrians, street vendors, conversations, traffic rhythms and ambient sounds.
4. **Distinctive cast:** faceless male fruit NPCs for the initial release, with coherent animation, clothing and accessories.
5. **Visual polish:** clear art language and scalable rendering.
6. **Expandable architecture:** discrete regions, reusable traffic/NPC/vehicle systems and content data.

## Anti-goals
- Full-size GTA V/VI quality, scale and game systems at launch.
- A 1:1 survey-quality copy of every road/building in Dhaka.
- Paid cloud APIs, paid assets or subscriptions beyond those already owned.
- An identical FPS or visual feature set on all browsers, tablets and phones.
- Mass-produced AI assets without artistic review and coherent scale/style.

## Success signals
First milestone: basic gameplay works and looks intentionally composed; testers recognize a Dhaka/Mirpur inspiration; stable repeatable performance on defined test hardware; no major clipping, collision or visual continuity defects. Later: retention, travel enjoyment, landmark recognition, completion of vehicle/NPC activities and educational evaluation only after missions exist.

## Assumptions to test
A recognizable location improves initial interest; players enjoy exploration without quests; browser loading can stay acceptable with high-quality assets; stylized fruit characters fit realistic Bangladesh environments. Treat all four as hypotheses until users play the demo.
